# VISUAL-RULES.md — Monkey Finance Image Prompt Visual Bible

## The One-Line Brief

Every scene should feel like it was drawn by a talented illustrator who read the script, understood the emotional moment, and drew exactly the right thing — bold, clean, and instantly readable at a glance.

---

## Style Foundation

**Style:** 2D colourful whiteboard animation style. Clean white background.
**Rendering:** Hand-drawn marker lines. Flat 2D. Slightly imperfect lines — adds authenticity.
**Forbidden:** No photorealism. No 3D rendering. No cinematic lighting. No gradients.

The aesthetic is: someone incredibly skilled drew this with colourful markers on a clean white surface. Everything visible — text, diagrams, characters, props — feels hand-crafted and direct.

---

## Colour Palette — Strict Roles

Every colour in every scene earns its place. Never use colour decoratively.

| Colour | Role | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Danger, loss, warning, tax, falling | HMRC, tax bills, bad news, negative stats, warnings |
| **Green** | Growth, gains, safety, solution, good news | Positive outcomes, solutions, savings growing, correct choices |
| **Yellow / Orange** | Emphasis, caution, spotlight, energy | Highlighting key terms, mid-level warnings, drawing attention |
| **Black** | Primary text, neutral diagrams, structure | Default for written stats, most diagrams, outlines |
| **Blue** | Calm, information, data, explanation | Background data, neutral context, explanatory diagrams |
| **White** | Background only | Never use white as a design element — it's the canvas |

**Rule:** Every scene should use a maximum of 3 colours. More than 3 = visual noise.

## Canvas Composition Principles

### The Rule of Halves
Most scenes work best split into two zones:
- **Left:** The data, diagram, stat, or concept
- **Right:** The monkey (if present), or a secondary visual element

If no monkey is present, the visual can span the full canvas — but leave breathing room.

### Focal Hierarchy
Every prompt must have one dominant element the eye lands on first. Achieve this through:
- **Size:** The most important element is largest
- **Colour:** Red or yellow draws the eye before black
- **Position:** Centre or upper-left gets attention first

### Energy Direction
Use arrows, lines, and pointing to guide the viewer's eye through the composition.
- Arrows should always point toward the key information
- The monkey (when present) should always face or point toward the content, not away from it

## Character Specifications

### Green Suit Monkey (Scene 01 and Final Scene ONLY)
- A simple monkey drawn in 2D hand-drawn style
- Wearing a bright green suit — the brand mascot in full presentation mode
- Expressive face — confident, welcoming, or energetic depending on scene context
- May hold props: a marker, a sign, a megaphone
- Always positioned on the right side of the frame, facing left (toward the content)
- Slightly larger than the monkey — this is the brand moment

### Monkey (Emotional scenes only — ~15–20% of scenes)
- Same monkey character, no suit — casual, unguarded, human
- Used only at moments of peak emotional reaction
- Expressive face is everything — the emotion must be readable at a glance:
  - **Shock/horror:** hands on head, mouth open, eyes wide
  - **Confusion/disbelief:** arms crossed, one eyebrow raised, dismissive expression
  - **Realisation:** pointing forward, eyes wide open, mouth forming an "O"
  - **Relief/satisfaction:** relaxed posture, slight smile, thumbs up
  - **Concern/worry:** hand on chin, furrowed brow, leaning forward
- May hold simple props: a thought bubble, a question mark, a coffee cup

### No Monkey (Most scenes — ~65–70% of scenes)
- Pure visual storytelling — diagrams, stats, timelines, comparisons, equations
- These scenes are often the most powerful — no character to distract from the data
- Content is centred, given full canvas space, and composed cleanly

---

## What Never Appears in Any Scene

- ❌ Logos of any kind (channel, brand, company)
- ❌ A physical whiteboard object (frame, tray, eraser, chalk ledge)
- ❌ Jungle environments, vines, tropical colours as primary setting
- ❌ 3D rendering or photorealistic elements
- ❌ Cinematic lighting effects
- ❌ Text that says "Monkey Finance" or any channel name
- ❌ Green suit monkey in any scene except Scene 01 and the final scene
- ❌ More than 3 colours in a single scene
- ❌ Cluttered compositions with more than 4 visual elements

## Common Visual Devices — Use Freely

These are your tools. Each one has a specific job.

| Device | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Threshold line** | Show a boundary or limit | The £325,000 IHT threshold |
| **Split-screen** | Show before/after or two options | Old rule vs new rule |
| **Bar chart** | Compare quantities | 10,500 vs 38,500 estates |
| **Timeline** | Show change over time | 30 years of saving, then a rule change |
| **Equation** | Show addition, combination, or cause/effect | House + Savings + ISA + Pension = Estate Total |
| **Fork in the road** | Show two choices | Old strategy vs new strategy |
| **Stacked layers** | Show compounding or combination | IHT + Income Tax = Double Tax |
| **Warning triangle** | Signal danger or urgent news | Tax bomb reveal |
| **Checklist** | Show action steps | Three things to do before April 2027 |
| **Thought bubble** | Show misconception or assumption | "This won't affect me" |
| **Explosion starburst** | Emphasise urgency or key stat | "40% TAX" callout |

## Writing the Visual Description

A good prompt answers four questions in order:

1. **What is the central visual?** (the main thing taking up most of the frame)
2. **What text/numbers appear?** (written in marker style on the canvas)
3. **Where is the monkey, if present?** (position, expression, what they're doing)
4. **What colours dominate?** (which palette roles are active in this scene)

Write in this order. Be specific. "A chart appears" is weak. "A simple hand-drawn bar chart with three bars — the tallest bar in red labeled '£34,000'" is strong.
