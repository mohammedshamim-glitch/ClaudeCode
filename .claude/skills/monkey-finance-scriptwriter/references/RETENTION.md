# RETENTION.md — Retention Science & Hook Architecture

## Why Viewers Leave (and How to Stop It)

Viewers don't leave because they're bored of your topic. They leave when they stop anticipating what's coming next.

The brain releases dopamine twice: when it anticipates an answer to an open question, and again when the answer arrives. A script that maintains high retention is one that manages this cycle deliberately — opening loops, holding them open long enough to create tension, then closing them with a satisfying payoff, immediately before opening the next one.

**The stats:**
- 55% of viewers are lost within the first 60 seconds when the opening fails to create a curiosity loop
- Videos using open loops see a 32% increase in watch time
- Pattern interrupts in the first 5 seconds produce a 23% higher retention rate on average
- Target: 50–60%+ average view duration — that's when YouTube starts recommending aggressively

---

## The Curiosity Loop System

Every great script is a chain of linked curiosity loops. Each loop has three parts:

**1. The Open** — raise a question or tension the viewer needs resolved
> *"In a moment, I'm going to tell you the one thing most financial advisers get completely wrong about this — but first..."*

**2. The Hold** — delay the answer long enough to build tension, but not so long the viewer gives up
> *(Continue building context, deepening the problem, adding stakes — the answer is coming but not yet)*

**3. The Close** — pay off the loop with a satisfying, specific answer
> *"Here's what they miss: [the actual insight]. And once you see it, you can't unsee it."*

**Rules for loops:**
- Never leave a loop open for more than 3–4 minutes of script without at least a partial payoff
- Always close one loop before opening the next — or the viewer feels cheated
- Plant the Grand Payoff loop in the hook and close it in Section 3 — this is the spine of the video
- Use 2–4 secondary loops across the body to maintain momentum

## Hook Architecture — The 3-Part Formula

Every hook must do all three of these within 20 seconds:

**Part 1: Validate the click** — confirm they're in exactly the right place
> *"If you've got a pension — and most people in the UK do — what I'm about to tell you affects you directly."*

**Part 2: Raise the stakes** — make the cost of not watching feel real
> *"Because a rule change coming next April could cost your family thousands of pounds — and almost nobody knows it's happening."*

**Part 3: Open the first curiosity loop** — hint at the Grand Payoff without revealing it
> *"By the end of this video, you'll know exactly what to do about it. But first — let me show you why this is bigger than most people realise."*

## The 8 Hook Formulas

### Formula 1: The Shocking Stat
Open with a number so surprising it stops the viewer cold.
> *"In less than twelve months, your pension could be taxed at 40% when you die. And right now, millions of UK savers have no idea it's coming."*
**Best for:** Tax changes, new laws, economic shifts with hard numbers.

---

### Formula 2: The Relatable Scenario
Drop them into a situation they immediately recognise.
> *"Imagine you're 54. You've spent thirty years paying into your pension, doing everything right. Then you find out a rule change means your family could lose a third of it before they see a penny. That's not hypothetical. That starts next April."*
**Best for:** Pension, savings, mortgage — anything emotionally charged.

---

### Formula 3: The Counterintuitive Claim
Say something that sounds wrong. They stay to find out why.
> *"Fixing your mortgage right now might be the worst financial decision you make this year. I know. Stay with me — because the reason is not what you think."*
**Best for:** Investment decisions, savings strategy, any topic where conventional wisdom can be challenged.

---

### Formula 4: The Urgent Window
Create a hard deadline that makes watching feel time-sensitive.
> *"Your ISA allowance just reset. You've got a £20,000 tax-free wrapper sitting there — and every month you leave it in a cash account earning 0.5%, you're losing money in real terms. Let's fix that today."*
**Best for:** ISA season, tax year deadlines, rate changes, anything time-sensitive.

---

### Formula 5: The Promise Hook
Lead with the exact, specific outcome. Make the value undeniable.
> *"By the end of this video, you'll know whether to fix your mortgage now or wait — using three questions that take five minutes to answer. No spreadsheet. No adviser. Just a clear decision you can make today."*
**Best for:** How-to guides, decision-making frameworks, practical explainers.

---

### Formula 6: The Pattern Interrupt
Start with something completely unexpected — a question, a reversal, an admission.
> *"The advice most financial influencers give you about ISAs is wrong. Not slightly wrong. Fundamentally, expensively wrong. And I'm going to show you exactly why."*
**Best for:** High-competition topics where you need to stand out from identical videos.

---

### Formula 7: The Stakes Escalator
Build stakes in three quick beats, each one bigger than the last.
> *"This affects your mortgage. It affects your savings. And if you've got a pension — it could cost your family more than you've ever imagined. Here's what's changing."*
**Best for:** Macro economic topics, Bank of England decisions, budget changes.

---

### Formula 8: The Insider Reveal
Position the viewer as about to receive information most people don't have access to.
> *"Most people don't find out about this until they're sitting across from a financial adviser — and by then, it's often too late to do anything about it. Today, you're finding out now."*
**Best for:** Complex tax rules, estate planning, obscure but important financial mechanisms.

## In-Video Retention Techniques

### The Loop Open (use 2–3 per video)
Plant a question early that won't be answered until later. Keep the tension alive.
> *"We'll get to the most important thing you can do in a moment — but first, you need to understand why the old advice no longer applies."*
> *"I'll tell you the exact number in a minute — but the reason behind it is what really matters."*

---

### The Mini-Hook (end of every section, without fail)
The last line of every section must pull the viewer into the next one. Never let a section just... stop.

**Tier 1 — Curiosity:** *"But here's where it gets really interesting..."*
**Tier 2 — Revelation:** *"And that brings us to the part most people completely miss."*
**Tier 3 — Stakes:** *"What I'm about to tell you surprised even me."*
**Tier 4 — Escalation:** *"The third point is the one that changes everything."*
**Tier 5 — Callback:** *"Remember what I said at the start? This is why it matters."*

---

### The Empathy Moment (once per video, mid-way through)
Acknowledge the viewer's feeling. It builds trust and re-engages drifters.
> *"I know — this is starting to feel complex. But I promise, it's simpler than it looks. And the payoff is worth it."*
> *"If you're watching this thinking 'I've already made this mistake' — don't panic. There's still time."*

---

### The Payoff Callback (once, in takeaways)
Return to the hook's open loop and close it completely. Gives the viewer a satisfying sense of journey.
> *"Remember that figure I opened with — the one about families losing £34,000? Now you know exactly how to make sure that doesn't happen to yours."*

---

### Value Ascending Rule
Each point in Section 3 must be more valuable than the previous one. Structure your material with the best saved for last — not front-loaded.

> Beat 1: Useful
> Beat 2: Really useful
> Beat 3: The thing they'll tell their partner about tonight

If your material is all equal, find a way to sequence it so it feels like it's building.

## Hook Mistakes — The Instant Killers

**The slow open:** *"Hey guys, welcome back to Monkey Finance, so today we're going to be talking about..."* — NEVER. Every word before "impact" is a viewer lost.

**The vague tease:** *"In this video, I'm going to share something really important..."* — Means nothing. Be specific or be ignored.

**The credentials open:** *"My name is X and I've been in finance for Y years..."* — Nobody cares yet. Earn authority through content, not introduction.

**The academic opener:** *"Inheritance tax, also known as IHT, is a levy imposed on estates..."* — This is a Wikipedia article, not a script. Start with the human story.

**The false alarm:** Overhyping something minor. Destroys trust permanently — the viewer won't come back.

**The long hook:** More than 100 words before the pivot into the problem. Trim it. Ruthlessly.
