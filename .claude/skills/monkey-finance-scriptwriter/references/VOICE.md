# VOICE.md — The Monkey Finance Voice

## The One-Line Brief

Write like the smartest person your viewer knows — the one who actually gets money — is explaining it to them over a pint. Warm. Direct. Occasionally funny. Never talking down to them.

---

## The Three Tones (Use All Three — They Alternate)

**Storytelling-led** — Finance is dry. Stories aren't. Every concept lives inside a narrative. Even a tax rule becomes a journey: here's the world as it was, here's what changed, here's what it means for you, here's what to do about it. Viewers don't remember bullet points. They remember stories.

**Relatable & conversational** — Write how a sharp person speaks, not how a financial white paper reads. Contractions. Direct address. Short sentences mixed with longer ones. The viewer should feel the script was written specifically for them — because in a sense it was.

**Energetic & punchy** — No waffle. No throat-clearing. Strong verbs, not weak nouns. "The government taxed it" not "a tax was applied." If a sentence can be cut without losing meaning, cut it. Every word earns its place or it's gone.

---

## The Monkey Theme — Precision Usage

The brand is Monkey Finance. It gives the channel warmth and personality. Use it sparingly so it lands every time.

### ✅ What works
- Natural wordplay: *"let's stop monkeying around and get your money working harder"*
- The channel sign-off: *"that's it from Monkey Finance — see you in the next one"*
- Opening personality beat: *"here at Monkey Finance, no suits, no jargon — just your money, explained"*
- A clever one-liner mid-video when it flows naturally: *"turns out, the smartest move in the room doesn't require a suit — just a bit of knowledge"*

### ❌ What kills it
- Forcing a monkey reference into a section where it doesn't fit — kills the flow
- "Troop" — do not use
- "Bananas" or jungle metaphors — archived, never use
- More than 2 references per video — becomes a gimmick

**Rule:** One reference in the opening or early hook area. One in the sign-off. That's your budget. Spend it wisely.

## Language — UK-First, Always

This is non-negotiable. Every single reference must be UK-relevant.

| ❌ Never say | ✅ Always say |
|---|---|
| 401k | Pension / SIPP |
| Retirement account | Pension |
| IRA | ISA / SIPP |
| Social Security | State Pension |
| The IRS | HMRC |
| Math | Maths |
| Realized / Analyze | Realised / Analyse |

## Sentence Architecture

**Vary relentlessly.** The rhythm of the script is what keeps ears engaged. Monotone sentence length = viewers drifting.

The ideal pattern: short punchy sentence. Slightly longer sentence that builds on it. Then a longer flowing one that pays it off. Then short again.

Example:
> "Most people have no idea this is coming. They've done everything right — saved into their pension, bought their house, paid their taxes. And then the rules change. Just like that, thousands of pounds gone."

**Use the active voice.** Always. "The government changed the rules" not "the rules were changed by the government." Passive voice is weak and distant.

**Ask rhetorical questions.** They're a conversation. They make the viewer feel heard.
- *"Sound familiar?"*
- *"So what does that actually mean for your money?"*
- *"You'd think that would be simple, right?"*

Use 3–5 per script. Space them out.

---

## Power Phrases — Use Freely

These signal transitions, build trust, and keep the tone conversational:

- *"Here's the thing..."*
- *"Let me explain..."*
- *"Think of it this way..."*
- *"The truth is..."*
- *"Here's what most people get wrong..."*
- *"And that's exactly the problem."*
- *"Stick with me — this is where it gets interesting."*
- *"Now here's the bit that surprises most people."*
- *"Bear with me on this one."*
- *"In plain English..."*
- *"What that actually means for you is..."*

---

## Phrases to Kill on Sight

These sound like essays, press releases, or robots. Delete immediately:

- "In conclusion" / "To summarise"
- "It is important to note that"
- "As previously mentioned"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Game-changer" / "Revolutionary" / "Unprecedented"
- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Moving forward"
- "With that being said"
- "Without further ado"
- Any sentence that starts with "So basically"

---

## Analogies — The Secret Weapon

The best analogies are:
1. Rooted in everyday UK life — not Wall Street, not Silicon Valley
2. Instantly visual — the viewer can see it without effort
3. Slightly unexpected — makes them memorable

**Examples of excellent analogies:**
- Compound interest: *"It's like a snowball rolling downhill. The longer it rolls, the bigger it gets — without you having to push it."*
- Diversification: *"You wouldn't put your whole weekly shop in one bag and hope the handles hold."*
- ISA allowance: *"Think of it as your tax-free wrapper. Anything inside is protected. Anything outside? Fair game for HMRC."*
- Inflation eroding savings: *"Leaving money in a 0.5% account when inflation is at 3% isn't safe — it's losing. Slowly, quietly, every month."*
- Pound-cost averaging: *"You're buying more shares when they're cheap and fewer when they're expensive — automatically, without thinking about it."*

**The mum test:** If you can't explain it to your mum in one sentence using an analogy, the analogy isn't ready. Keep working it.

## Emotional Architecture

Every script rides one dominant emotional arc. The opening creates the emotion. The content resolves it.

| Topic type | Emotional journey |
|---|---|
| Tax / law change | Fear → understanding → relief (I know what to do now) |
| Investing guide | Aspiration → clarity → confidence (I can do this) |
| ISA / pension explainer | Confusion → clarity → empowerment (I'm not behind) |
| Market volatility | Anxiety → calm → action (Here's my plan) |
| Savings / rates | Frustration → opportunity → motivation (I can fix this today) |
| Cautionary tale | Curiosity → concern → resolution (I won't make that mistake) |

Identify the arc before writing. The hook opens the emotion. The CTA closes it.
