# STRUCTURE.md — Script Blueprint & Pacing System

## The Core Principle: Value Must Escalate

Viewers are constantly and unconsciously asking: *"Is this still worth my time?"*

If the answer ever dips to "maybe not" — they're gone. The script must be designed so every section is more compelling than the last. Never front-load your best material. Build toward it.

**The Grand Payoff** — before writing, identify the single most satisfying moment the viewer clicked to see. Everything else in the script exists to build toward and pay off that moment. If you can't name it in one sentence, the script isn't ready.

---

## The Blueprint

Target: **1,400–1,600 words** | **10–12 minutes** | Pure narration, no visual cues

---

### SECTION 0: The Hook *(written last, delivered first)*
**~100 words | 0:00–0:45**

**Job:** Make it psychologically impossible to click away.

The hook has 20 seconds to do exactly three things:
1. **Validate the click** — confirm the viewer is in the right place
2. **Raise the stakes** — why this matters *right now*
3. **Open the first curiosity loop** — hint at the Grand Payoff without giving it away

**Rules:**
- Write it last — after the body exists, the hook almost writes itself
- Nothing longer than 10 words per sentence in the hook
- No warm-up lines. No "hey guys". No channel intro. Start mid-thought, start with impact.
- The hook ends by planting an open loop the viewer *needs* resolved — see `RETENTION.md`

**Transition out:** A pivot line that bridges into the problem without closing the loop.
> *"But before we get to that — you need to understand why most people are completely blindsided by this."*

---

### SECTION 1: The Problem *(why this matters to them personally)*
**~200 words | 0:45–2:15**

**Job:** Make the viewer emotionally invested before you explain anything.

This is the "so what?" section. The viewer needs to care — deeply — before they'll receive information. Don't start explaining yet. Stay in the problem. Make it real.

**Techniques:**
- Drop them into a scenario they recognise: *"Picture this. You're 54. You've done everything right..."*
- Use a concrete number or statistic that lands like a punch
- Name the villain — is it HMRC? Rising rates? A rule no one told them about?
- Use empathy: *"Most people only find out about this when it's too late."*

**What to avoid:** Starting the explanation here. The viewer hasn't earned it yet — and neither have you.

**Transition out:** A pivot that signals the explanation is coming.
> *"So let's break down exactly what's happening — and what you can do about it."*

---

### SECTION 2: The Foundation *(build the mental model)*
**~250 words | 2:15–4:30**

**Job:** Give the viewer everything they need to understand what follows — without them realising it's work.

This is the scaffolding section. You're building a mental model, one layer at a time. Complex topics need this. Don't skip it.

**Rules:**
- One concept at a time. Never introduce two new ideas in the same breath.
- Every new term gets a one-sentence plain English definition, then an analogy
- After each concept, confirm understanding: *"Make sense? Good."* or *"Stay with me."*
- Break into 2–3 mini-topics, ~80 words each

**Pacing trigger:** If a section feels like it's dragging, inject a rhetorical question or a surprising micro-fact to reset attention.

**Transition out:** Signal that the main content is approaching, and raise anticipation.
> *"Now here's where it gets really interesting — because this is the part most people completely miss."*

---

### SECTION 3: The Core Content *(the heart of the video)*
**~500 words | 4:30–8:30**

**Job:** Deliver the Grand Payoff. This is what they came for.

Structure as 3–4 distinct beats, each opening and closing its own mini curiosity loop. Value must escalate — beat 3 must be more impactful than beat 1.

**For each beat:**
- Open with a clear statement: *"Here's the first thing you need to know..."*
- Make it concrete with a story, real-world example, or analogy
- Close with the personal implication: *"What that means for you is..."*
- End with a mini-hook into the next beat: *"But that's only half the picture."*

**Retention trigger — use at least twice in this section:**
> Every 90 seconds, inject one of:
> - A surprising counter-intuitive insight (*"Most people do X — but actually Y is the smarter move"*)
> - A rhetorical question (*"Sound familiar? You're not alone."*)
> - A brief moment of personality or light humour
> - A callback to the hook's open loop (*"Remember what I said at the start? Here's why that matters."*)

**The Payoff Moment:** Somewhere in this section — usually beat 3 — deliver the Grand Payoff. This is the insight the viewer will screenshot, share, or tell someone about. Make it land.

**Transition out:** Signal the end is near, but frame it as reward not conclusion.
> *"So — what does all of this actually mean, and what should you do about it?"*

---

### SECTION 4: Key Takeaways *(crystallise and activate)*
**~200 words | 8:30–10:00**

**Job:** Transform what they've learned into actions they'll take this week.

Never just summarise. A summary is passive. Takeaways are active — they change what the viewer does next.

**Format:** 3–5 takeaways. Each gets:
- A number or signal word: *"First... Second... The most important thing..."*
- One specific, actionable instruction (not "think about your pension" — instead "check if your pension is defined contribution or defined benefit — it changes everything")
- One sentence that makes it memorable

**The Callback:** Close takeaways with a callback to the hook's open loop — pay it off completely.
> *"Remember that number I opened with? Now you know exactly what it means — and what to do about it."*

**Transition out:** A warm, earned confidence-builder.
> *"You're already ahead of most people just by knowing this."*

---

### SECTION 5: CTA & Sign-Off *(earn the subscription)*
**~120 words | 10:00–11:00**

**Job:** Convert a satisfied viewer into a subscriber. Feel earned, not bolted on.

The viewer just received real value. Now — and only now — is the moment to ask for something back. The CTA must feel like a natural continuation, not a commercial break.

**Structure:**
1. **One-line value acknowledgement** — validate what they just learned (*"If this video just saved your family from a £34,000 tax bill they didn't know was coming..."*)
2. **The ask** — direct, confident, no desperation (*"Hit subscribe — we put out new videos every week breaking down your money in plain English"*)
3. **Sign-off** — warm, on-brand, final

**Example sign-off:**
> *"Until then — this is Monkey Finance. See you in the next one."*

**What to avoid:** Begging for likes. Multiple CTAs. A sign-off that sounds tired. End with energy.

---

## Pacing Rules

**The 90-second rule:** Never let more than 90 seconds pass without a retention trigger — a surprise, a question, a revelation, a callback, or a moment of personality.

**Section length variance:** Sections 0–2 build slowly (engagement over information). Section 3 moves fast (information, impact, payoff). Sections 4–5 are clean and crisp.

**Paragraph length:** 3–4 spoken sentences maximum per paragraph. Shorter paragraphs = more breathing room = easier to listen to.

**The aloud test:** Read every section aloud before finalising. If you stumble, the listener will stumble. Rewrite until it flows like speech, not text.
